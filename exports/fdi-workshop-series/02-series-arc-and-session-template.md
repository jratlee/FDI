# 02 · Series Arc & Session Template

## The season arc

**The Cartographers' Table** runs as a **four-part season** that mirrors the FDI
thesis exactly: one session to name the problem, then one session per market
(Aggregated, Decentralized, Autonomous). Each session builds one more layer of a
real owned system, so the artifacts stack.

| # | Session title | Maps to | What the attendee builds (keeps) | The next-step it opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **The Terrain: Build the Machine, Not the Ad** | The thesis / diagnosis | A one-page map of where their (or a client's) marketing is renting reach vs. owning a system | The Field Guide + free Top Call prompt-pack |
| **1** | **Aggregated: Become the Answer the Machine Cites** *(FLAGSHIP)* | Aggregated market | One graded source list + one owned **answer object** for a real high-intent question | SkillFoundry (source-authority grading as repeatable code) |
| **2** | **Decentralized: Turn Scattered Knowledge into an Owned Graph** | Decentralized market | A hand-drawn mini knowledge graph of one client's entities and relationships | SkillFoundry / Top Call graph layer |
| **3** | **Autonomous: Make Your System Something an Agent Can Trust** | Autonomous market | A spec for one "tool call" an agent could make against their system, with trust boundaries | Top Call MCP interface + SkillFoundry Tier 2/3 |

**Session 1 is the flagship** and the one fully kitted in `03` and `04`. It is the
best standalone entry point: the citation audit is the most visceral demonstration
of the thesis, and it maps most directly onto the SkillFoundry Tier 1 offer, so a
newcomer who attends only Session 1 still gets a complete, satisfying loop.

### What makes people come back (the compounding hook)

The retention mechanic is the thesis, made physical:

1. **The artifacts stack into one system.** Session 1's answer object becomes a
   node in Session 2's graph, which becomes a callable tool in Session 3's agent
   spec. Miss a session and your system has a hole; attend the season and you walk
   away with a coherent, owned asset. This is the single strongest reason to return.
2. **The room demonstrates its own thesis.** Attending one session is a *rented
   outcome*: a useful artifact that does not compound. Attending the season is an
   *owned system*. Facilitators name this out loud. The workshop practices what it
   preaches, which is memorable and quotable.
3. **A living scoreboard.** Each session opens by revisiting the previous session's
   thesis-validation vote (see `06`) and any attendee wins ("who got cited by an
   LLM since last time?"). Social proof compounds alongside the artifacts.
4. **A named cohort.** Returning attendees become "the Table" for that city, a
   standing peer group of AI-forward strategists. Belonging is a return force that
   a one-off event never earns.

### Cadence guidance (no dates set here)

- **Monthly** per season (four months = one full season), then repeat the season
  in a new city or with a new cohort. Monthly is frequent enough to keep the
  artifacts fresh in mind and infrequent enough for busy independents.
- Re-run **Session 1 as a standalone recurring "open table"** between seasons to
  keep filling the top of the funnel with newcomers, since it is the cleanest
  entry point.

## The reusable single-session template

Every session, regardless of theme, follows the **same seven-beat spine** so a
facilitator can re-skin it in an afternoon. Only the middle three beats (the map,
the build, the tool bridge) change per theme.

| Beat | Purpose | Time (of a 2.5 hr session) | Changes per session? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Arrival & capture** | Name tags, table tents, scan the QR, coffee | 15 min | No (same capture, see `05`) |
| **2 · The cold open** | One vivid live demonstration of the problem for *this* market | 15 min | **Yes** — the theme's "shock moment" |
| **3 · The map** | Short teach: the FDI framing for this market, grounded in the Field Guide | 20 min | **Yes** — thesis chapter for this market |
| **4 · The build (hands-on)** | Attendees produce one real artifact for a real client, in pairs | 55 min | **Yes** — the exercise + worksheet |
| **5 · The share** | 3 to 4 attendees show their artifact; facilitator reacts | 20 min | No (same format) |
| **6 · The bridge** | "You did this once, by hand. Here is how you make it repeatable and provable." Live SkillFoundry / Top Call moment | 15 min | **Yes** — which product, which feature |
| **7 · The exit ticket & close** | Thesis-validation vote + exit card + next-session tease | 10 min | No (same instrument, see `06`) |

Beats 1, 5, and 7 are **fixed infrastructure** you build once. Beats 2, 3, 4, and
6 are the **skin** you swap per theme. To design a new session, a facilitator only
has to answer four questions:

1. **Cold open:** what is the one live thing that makes this market's problem
   undeniable in 90 seconds?
2. **Map:** which Field Guide passage frames it, and what is the single diagram?
3. **Build:** what is the one artifact they can produce in 55 minutes for a real
   client, and what worksheet scaffolds it?
4. **Bridge:** which product feature turns that by-hand artifact into owned,
   repeatable, provable code?

The flagship session (`03`) is the fully worked example of all four answers.

## Format variants (same spine, different length)

- **90-minute lunch-and-learn:** compress beats 3 to 6, single-question build,
  skip the share round-robin (facilitator narrates two examples instead).
- **Half-day intensive:** run two adjacent themes back to back (e.g., Aggregated +
  Decentralized) so attendees leave with an answer object *and* the graph it feeds.
- **Live-remote cohort:** identical spine over video; the build happens in
  breakout pairs, the share uses screen-share, capture is a link not a QR. This is
  the fallback if the target segment is too geographically sparse (see `01`).

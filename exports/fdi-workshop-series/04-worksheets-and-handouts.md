## Handout B — Source-Authority quick-reference card (1 page)

> **How a strategist grades what a machine should trust**
> Print double-sided or half-page. Attendees keep this.

**Tier 1 · Own it.** First-party, named, dated, verifiable. Primary sources,
original research, the practitioner or brand speaking for itself on an owned
property. *Test: could you defend it by name in a client meeting and prove who said
it first?*

**Tier 2 · Corroborated.** Reputable independent coverage, established publications,
recognized experts citing checkable evidence. Trustworthy but not yours. *Test: is
it independent and does it show its evidence?*

**Tier 3 · Noise.** Vendor SEO filler, undated listicles, unattributed roundups,
anything optimized to rank rather than to be true. *Test: would you quietly discount
it the moment you saw the persuasion machinery?*

**The grading questions (apply to any source):**

1. **Named?** Is there an accountable author or organization behind it?
2. **Dated?** Do you know when it was true, and is it still?
3. **Verifiable?** Can the core claim be independently checked?
4. **Owned?** Is it first-party, or is it renting someone else's authority?
5. **Provable origin?** Could a machine tell who said it first?

**The owned-systems reminder:** grading you do once, by hand, evaporates when the
chat ends. Grading encoded as repeatable, provable code compounds. The recipe is
not the moat; the owned, graded corpus is.

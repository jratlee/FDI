# 01 · Audience Decision (Design-Thinking)

**Challenge:** Choose ONE primary in-room audience for a recurring FDI workshop
that generates SkillFoundry leads, builds thesis authority, teaches a hands-on
skill, and validates the owned-marketing-systems thesis.

## Framework used

**JTBD Switch Interview + Adoption Forces**, run in **Standard mode**. FDI already
has customers and a validated beachhead (the SkillFoundry Tier 1 prospect
research), so the useful question is not "who might we invent a persona for" but
"who is *already switching* toward owned systems, and what forces move them." That
is a JTBD question, not a blank-slate Double Diamond one.

## The four candidate audiences

1. **Founders / operators** (startup CEOs, solo founders doing their own growth)
2. **In-house marketers / growth teams** (employed content, comms, growth staff)
3. **Agencies / consultants** (fractional CMOs, independent strategists, boutique shops)
4. **A mixed conference room** (whoever shows up at a general marketing event)

## Problem definition

### HMW statement

> How might we give AI-forward independent marketing strategists a room where they
> personally build one durable, provable piece of an owned system, so they leave
> convinced that *strategy they own beats execution they rent* and want the tool
> that makes it repeatable?

Scope check: not so narrow that it bakes in the solution ("HMW demo SkillFoundry"),
not so broad it is unactionable ("HMW teach marketers about AI"). It names the
user, the pain, and the shift we want.

### Key insights (evidence-grounded)

> **Insight 1 — The buyer whose job is *strategy* feels the thesis in their body,
> not just their head.**
> **Evidence:** The Tier 1 positioning brief finds the recurring pain across
> fractional CMOs and independent strategists is "execution is commoditized; my
> strategy is the only moat," and "strategy is trapped in my head or a slide deck."
> They re-build the same frameworks per client with no way to make judgment
> repeatable or provable.
> **Implication:** A workshop where they encode one piece of strategy as a durable,
> provable artifact is not a lecture about the thesis; it is direct relief of a
> pain they carry daily. The room sells itself.

> **Insight 2 — This segment already lives in the vocabulary, so the workshop needs
> zero education tax.**
> **Evidence:** The brief describes the beachhead as people who publicly build
> their practice around AI search (GEO / AEO), AI-native GTM, and AI agents, for
> whom "strategy as code over the open Model Context Protocol lands with zero
> education." AI search rewriting the game ("will an LLM cite us?") is a live, dated
> pain for them.
> **Implication:** We can go straight to hands-on building in the first session
> instead of spending an hour defining terms. A 2 to 3 hour session can produce a
> real artifact, which is what makes attendees advocates.

> **Insight 3 — Each attendee is a distribution node, so one filled room seeds the
> other two revenue tiers.**
> **Evidence:** The brief notes nearly every Tier 1 name runs a newsletter,
> podcast, community, or heavy LinkedIn presence, and that "one convinced Tier 1
> user becomes distribution into the in-house (Tier 2) and enterprise / agency
> (Tier 3) motions."
> **Implication:** A workshop optimized for this segment has leverage the other
> three audiences do not. Founders churn and go quiet; in-house staff cannot
> expense a $29 tool without procurement; a mixed room dilutes the message. The
> consultant room is both the warmest buyer *and* the loudest amplifier.

## Solution concepts (the four audiences as concepts)

| Concept (audience) | Desirable? | Feasible? | Viable? | Riskiest assumption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agencies / consultants (fractional CMOs + independent strategists)** | High — they feel the pain daily and speak the language | High — no education tax, they bring their own client cases | High — instant $29 self-serve buyers *and* referral nodes into Tier 2/3 | That enough of them are reachable in one metro to fill a recurring room |
| Founders / operators | Medium — pain is real but diffuse; growth is one of ten fires | Medium — wide skill range slows the hands-on part | Low/Med — buy fast but churn; weak referral into Tier 2/3 | That they will return for a *series* rather than one-off tactics |
| In-house marketers / growth teams | Medium — same pain at team scale | Medium — need laptops, sometimes IT constraints | Low for Tier 1 self-serve (procurement blocks $29 impulse); better as Tier 2 | That an employee can act on what they build without a budget cycle |
| Mixed conference room | Low — message must be watered down for the least-informed attendee | High to fill, low to satisfy | Low — no coherent funnel, weak signal | That a diluted message still converts anyone |

## Adoption forces — recommended concept (agencies / consultants)

| Force | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Push (pain of status quo)** | Strong. Execution is being automated out from under them; their strategy lives in their head and does not compound or prove itself; clients are panicking about AI search. The ground is moving. |
| **Pull (appeal of the workshop)** | Strong. "Leave today having built one owned, provable answer object for a real client, and learn the method you can repeat." Tangible, career-relevant, status-conferring (they can teach it to clients). |
| **Anxiety (fears about switching)** | Moderate. "Is this just a sales pitch for a tool?" and "will I actually build something, or just listen?" Both are designed out: the room is 60%+ hands-on, and the tool is positioned as *repeating what you did by hand today*, not replacing your judgment. |
| **Habit (comfort of current way)** | Moderate. Slide decks and Notion frameworks are good enough until AI search erodes them. The session makes the erosion vivid (the live citation audit) so "good enough" stops feeling good enough. |
| **Balance** | **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit.** The forces tilt toward adoption, and both blocking forces are directly addressable inside the room design. |

## Recommendation

**Run the series for AI-forward fractional CMOs and independent B2B marketing /
growth strategists (solo or 1 to 15 person practices serving SaaS and tech).** Do
not hedge across all four audiences. Founders and in-house teams are real future
markets but belong to Tier 1 upgrade paths and later series variants, not the
flagship room. A mixed conference room is explicitly rejected: it forces a diluted
message and breaks the funnel.

This choice also gives the series a clean growth loop: the room is the warmest
possible SkillFoundry Tier 1 buyer, and every attendee is a referral node into the
Tier 2 (in-house) and Tier 3 (enterprise / agency retainer) motions the
positioning brief already maps.

### Conviction level

**High.** The audience is not a fresh hypothesis; it is the beachhead FDI has
already researched, chosen, and built pricing around. The workshop simply gives
that beachhead an in-person, high-trust, high-amplification surface.

- **Would increase to Very High if:** a first pilot session fills to 12 or more of
  the target segment and 3 or more convert to a SkillFoundry action (buy, book a
  demo, or join the paid waitlist) within 10 days.
- **Would decrease if:** the target segment turns out to be too geographically
  sparse to fill a recurring room in one metro, in which case the same kit runs as
  a live-remote cohort instead (the exercises are laptop-friendly and portable).

## Prototype plan

- **What to build (lowest fidelity):** run the flagship session (`03`) once as a
  free 2.5 hour evening event for 8 to 15 hand-picked local strategists.
- **Who to test with:** 5+ of the target segment recruited directly (LinkedIn DMs,
  the existing waitlist, referrals from any current SkillFoundry contacts).
- **Success looks like:** every attendee leaves with a completed "answer object"
  worksheet; 70%+ scan the capture QR; 3+ take a SkillFoundry action within 10
  days; exit-ticket thesis score (see `06`) trends up versus entry.

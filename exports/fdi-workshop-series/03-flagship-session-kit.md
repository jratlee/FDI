# 03 · Flagship Session Kit

## Session 1 — Aggregated: Become the Answer the Machine Cites

**Audience:** AI-forward fractional CMOs and independent B2B strategists (see `01`).
**Length:** 2.5 hours (150 min). **Room:** 8 to 20 people, round tables of 3 to 4.
**Facilitator:** 1 lead + 1 optional floater during the build.
**One-sentence promise (put on the invite):** *"Leave tonight having built one
owned answer object for a real client that an AI assistant should cite, and the
repeatable method behind it."*

**The single takeaway skill:** run a **citation audit** on any high-intent question
in a category, grade the sources an AI currently cites, then draft the owned
**answer object** (a direct-answer definition + proof + provenance) that deserves
to be cited instead. This is the by-hand version of what SkillFoundry does as
verifiable, repeatable code.

## Facilitator script & talking points

Use these as talking points, not a read-aloud script. Keep the FDI master brand
and the Growth Cartography framing leading. No em-dashes needed when spoken; the
notes below use plain phrasing you can deliver naturally.

### Beat 1 — Arrival (0:00)

> "Welcome to the Cartographers' Table. Grab a name tag, grab coffee, and scan the
> code on your table tent so we can send you tonight's worksheet and the follow-up.
> Find a table with two or three others. In a few minutes we are going to draw a
> map of territory that did not exist five years ago."

Nudge every arrival to scan the QR now (capture rate is highest before the content
starts). The floater watches for anyone who did not scan.

### Beat 2 — Cold open (0:15)

> "Quick one. Someone shout out a question a real buyer in your world would ask an
> AI assistant. Not Google. Ask an assistant. Something like 'what is the best
> tool for X' or 'how do I solve Y.'"

Type the best one live. Read the answer aloud. Then the key move:

> "Now watch this part. Where did that answer come from? Whose content did the
> machine decide to trust and cite? Raise your hand if it cited you or a client of
> yours."

Almost no hands. Let the silence sit.

> "That is the whole night in one moment. For thirty years marketing was a bet on
> reach. That bet is over. The machine is now the front door, and it just decided,
> in front of all of us, who exists and who does not. Tonight you are going to
> build the thing that gets you through that door: not an ad, an answer it cites."

### Beat 3 — The map (0:30)

Teach the Aggregated chapter. Core beats to hit:

- The cost of making content just fell to zero. That is not the opportunity, that
  is the emergency. If content is free to make, making it is worth nothing. What
  stays scarce is provenance, trust, and the system that decides who gets seen.
- Discovery is moving behind recommenders and chat assistants. Your customer asks
  a model, not a search box, and the model decides whether you exist.
- You do not get to negotiate reach with an aggregator. What you *can* own is a
  persistent identity and a corpus of trustworthy, citable answers.
- The reframe to leave them with: **being found is rented; being cited is owned.**
  An SEO ranking is a position you rent from an algorithm that changes weekly. An
  answer object a model trusts and cites is an asset you own and can prove.

One diagram only (see `07`, Slide 5): a buyer's question flowing into a model, and
the model reaching past a wall of "renters" to cite one "owner."

Land the transition into the build:

> "So here is tonight's skill. We are going to pick one real question, audit who
> the machine trusts today, grade those sources the way a strategist should, and
> then build the owned answer that deserves the citation. You will leave with it."

### Beat 4 — The build (0:50)

Set it up in 3 minutes, then get out of the way. Read the worksheet's three parts
aloud (see `04`), set a visible timer, and circulate.

- Part A (15 min): choose the question, run the audit, list what AI cites now.
- Part B (15 min): grade each cited source on the authority rubric.
- Part C (25 min): draft the owned answer object.

Floater prompts while circulating (use these to unstick pairs):

- Stuck on the question: "What is the single highest-intent question a buyer asks
  right before they choose a vendor like your client?"
- Stuck on grading: "Would you stake your reputation on that source in a client
  meeting? If not, what tier is it really?"
- Stuck on the answer object: "Say the true answer in one sentence a twelve year
  old could follow. Now, what is the one proof that makes it undeniable, and how
  would a machine verify who said it first?"

Two-minute warning before each part ends.

### Beat 5 — The share (1:45)

> "Let us hear four. Read me your one-sentence answer, then tell me who is winning
> the citation today and what tier they actually deserve."

React with specifics. Praise a sharp definition, tighten a vague one live, and
call out any answer object that carries real provenance. This is where the room
learns from each other and where you seed testimonials.

### Beat 6 — The bridge (2:05)

> "Look at what you just did. You graded source authority. You wrote an answer with
> proof and provenance. That is strategy, and it is exactly the strategy that is
> your moat now that execution is free. But there is a catch, and you already feel
> it. You did this once, for one question, and the second you close that notebook
> it starts to evaporate. Next week you re-audit from scratch. Nothing compounds,
> nothing is provable to a client, and an agent cannot call it."

> "That gap is what we build at False Dawn. SkillFoundry takes exactly what you did
> tonight and makes it durable: source-authority grading as repeatable code, every
> answer stamped with where it came from, over an open interface an agent can query
> and trust. You own the system, you do not rent the outcome."

Show the SkillFoundry grading gate and a verifiable output for 3 to 4 minutes. Do
not oversell. The point already landed in their hands during the build.

> "The prompt-pack we will email you is the free, by-hand version of this. It works.
> It also shows you its own ceiling, which is the whole point."

### Beat 7 — Exit ticket & close (2:20)

Run the thesis-validation vote and exit card from `06`. Then:

> "Next month at this table: Decentralized. The answer object you built tonight
> becomes one node in an owned knowledge graph of your client's whole world. Bring
> it back. Check your inbox tonight for the worksheet, the Field Guide, and the free
> prompt-pack. Thank you for drawing the map with us."

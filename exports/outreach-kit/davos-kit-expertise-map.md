# Davos Decision Kit: Where Your Team's Expertise Goes In

**For:** Heather Kernahan, The Content Bureau
**From:** False Dawn Industries

The kit's credibility is your expertise. FDI provides the structure: the
scoring math, the document architecture, the packaging, the commerce
plumbing. Your team provides the truth: what the ranges really are, when the
calendars really fill, which scripts really get replies. Neither works alone.
This page maps every point in the 2 to 3 week build where that truth gets
inserted, and what it costs your team in hours.

**Total time required from your team: about 4 to 6 hours across the build.**

## The insertion map

| When | What FDI brings | What TCB inserts | Why it matters | Your time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1: the working session (the big one) | Draft scorecard with six weighted factors, draft budget lines from public ranges, draft twelve-month runway | Validate the weights and thresholds against real client outcomes: which factors actually predicted a good week, where the go/no-go line really sits. Correct budget ranges from your ground truth. Correct runway timing: when side-event lists really close, when senior calendars really fill. Your voice and point of view on the badge question | A scorecard with wrong weights produces confident wrong answers. Public ranges without practitioner correction read like a blog post, not senior advice | 90 to 120 minutes, one scheduled session |
| Week 1: async follow-ups | The visibility templates: script skeletons, briefing-doc structure, model-week grid, follow-up cadence | Real, anonymized patterns: which meeting-request framings get replies from that audience, the model week as your practice actually runs it, the follow-up cadence you have seen convert | Scripts are the part buyers copy verbatim. If they sound like generic outreach, the kit reads as generic. Your patterns are the product | 60 to 90 minutes of async review, notes in the margins are enough |
| Week 2: the red-line pass | The full revised kit, incorporating everything above | A red-line for three things: anything that overpromises, anything that conflicts with your advisory positioning, and anything that leaks proprietary method you want kept behind the advisory door | The kit must qualify buyers for advisory, not replace it. You decide where the line between "in the kit" and "in the room" sits | 60 to 90 minutes, one pass |
| Week 2: sign-off | Final packaged kit, launch copy, product page draft | A yes or a short punch list | Nothing ships under your brand without your final word | 15 to 30 minutes |
| Week 3 (optional add-on): commerce check | Checkout, gated download, and email capture wired and tested end to end | One test purchase walkthrough with us on a screen share | You should see exactly what a buyer sees before a buyer sees it | 30 minutes |

## Why the insertions are structured this way

- **One big session, not many small ones.** Everything that needs live
  conversation is concentrated in the Week 1 working session. Everything else
  is async and fits between meetings.
- **FDI drafts first, your team corrects.** Correcting a draft takes a
  fraction of the time that authoring does. Your hours go where they are
  irreplaceable: judgment, ranges, and voice.
- **The red-line is a veto, not a rewrite.** By Week 2 the kit already
  reflects your session and async input; the red-line exists to catch what
  only you can catch.
- **Nothing proprietary leaves without consent.** The default is
  conservative: anything close to your advisory method stays out of the kit
  until you explicitly put it in.

# We are opening a lab where an AI agent answers your growth questions with math, not vibes

*A LinkedIn article for the Open Cartography Lab launch. Author: John Ratcliffe-Lee, False Dawn Industries.*

---

Every growth conversation I have ends the same way. Someone asks a question that has a computable answer, and gets an opinion instead.

"Is our CAC defensible?" That is arithmetic. "Where does our user base stabilize if churn stays at 8%?" That is a formula. "Should we do the airdrop or the drip?" That is two curves on the same chart.

These questions do not need a consultant with a point of view. They need a model with your numbers in it.

So we built one, gave it a staff position, and put it in a public room.

## The Open Cartography Lab

The Lab is a public community with one unusual resident: the Growth Cartography Agent, an always-on AI staffer that answers growth-modeling questions by actually running the model.

You post a scenario with real numbers. Users, churn, acquisition rate, retention at day 1, 7, and 30. The agent parses it, runs a compounding cohort-decay engine, and replies with:

- The equilibrium your network is heading toward
- The assumptions it used, stated plainly
- The two or three levers that move the outcome most

Every answer is computed, not composed. When the agent cannot parse your scenario, it does not improvise. It asks for the missing parameters. And every exchange is a permanent, citable thread you can link in your next board deck.

## Why it works across three very different markets

The same cohort math describes three worlds that look unrelated on the surface:

**Aggregated markets.** Classic SaaS and consumer. Users, monthly churn, CAC versus LTV. The oldest version of the question.

**Decentralized markets.** Token networks and node counts. The airdrop-versus-drip question is a retention question wearing a costume. A 50,000-node airdrop at 6% day-30 retention loses to a 500-node daily drip within two months. The math does not care how exciting the launch event was.

**Autonomous markets.** Agents transacting with agents. Cost per onboarded agent, micro-transaction volume, break-even frontiers. The newest market with the oldest math underneath it.

Unit changes. Curve does not.

## Why we are not doing this on Discord

The Lab runs on a self-hosted relay built on open-source infrastructure (Buzz, an open-source project by Block). Every message is a cryptographically signed event tied to a keypair, not a platform account.

That choice is the whole thesis of False Dawn Industries in miniature: owned marketing systems. A community that lives on someone else's platform is a community you rent. The threads in the Lab are permanent, portable, and verifiable. Nobody's algorithm decides what you see. Nobody's policy team decides what stays.

We are building a research organization in public, and the Lab is where the public part happens.

## What it costs and who it is for

Free. No paywall for the first ring. The agent answers everyone who brings a real scenario with real numbers.

It is for founders modeling their next raise, growth leads sanity-checking a channel bet, token teams deciding between a spike and a drip, and anyone building in agent markets who wants the break-even math before the pitch deck says "conservative estimate."

The one house rule that matters: bring numbers. The agent cannot model a vibe.

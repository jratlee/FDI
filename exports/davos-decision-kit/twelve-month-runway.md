# The Twelve-Month Runway

Davos outcomes are decided months before the week begins. This is the
month-by-month plan, working backward from the January meeting. Adjust the
calendar to your cycle; the sequence matters more than the exact months.

## February (the year before): decide

- Run the Go/No-Go Scorecard while last cycle's lessons are fresh in the market
- Write the one-page recommendation and get the budget line approved in principle
- Decide the badge question early: badged participation, promenade-and-side-events, or hosted-moment-only. Each is a different plan and a different budget

## March to April: position

- Name the single point of view you will own that week. One idea, stated sharply, beats five talking points
- Audit the principal's public footprint: bylines, podcasts, keynotes, social presence. Gaps found now are fixable; gaps found in November are not
- Start the target list: the 40 to 60 people whose presence would justify the trip

## May to June: publish

- Begin publishing the point of view: a flagship byline or report, then a steady cadence. The people you want to meet should encounter the idea before they encounter you
- Map the side-event landscape from last cycle: which houses, forums, and dinners matched your themes, and who ran them
- If hosting a moment, book the venue now. Prime slots and spaces go early

## July to August: build access

- Convert the target list into warm paths: who on your board, in your investor base, or in your network can introduce you to each name
- Apply, register, or open conversations for the side events and forums that fit. Many curate guest lists months out
- Confirm lodging. Prices and availability only get worse

## September to October: sharpen

- Publish the anchor piece: the report, essay, or data story your January conversations will reference
- Draft the briefing doc for the week (template in `visibility-plan-templates.md`)
- Begin meeting-stack outreach for the week itself: senior calendars for that week fill from October
- Media: brief the journalists who cover your space and will be there; offer the anchor piece as background

## November: lock

- Lock the meeting stack: aim for 8 to 12 confirmed high-value conversations, held loosely enough to survive the week's chaos
- Confirm every side-event slot, dinner seat, and speaking moment in writing
- Production: finalize one-pagers, talk tracks, and the two-minute version of the point of view
- Logistics rehearsal: transfers, gear, backup plans for weather delays

## December: prepare the principal

- Brief the principal on every confirmed meeting: who, why, the one outcome that matters
- Rehearse the talk track until it is conversational, not recited
- Pre-draft the follow-up materials so they ship the same week, not in February
- Protect the calendar: the week before Davos should be light

## January: execute

- Work the model week in `visibility-plan-templates.md`
- Capture everything daily: who you met, what was promised, what surprised you
- Ship follow-ups within 72 hours of each conversation, while you are still a vivid memory

## February: convert

- Run the follow-up system to completion; the trip's ROI is decided here
- Score the week against the original one-page recommendation
- Make next year's go/no-go call while the evidence is fresh

---

General guidance based on practitioner experience, not legal or financial
advice. Event structures and timelines are set by the World Economic Forum
and event hosts and change year to year; verify current details. This kit is
independent and not affiliated with or endorsed by the World Economic Forum.

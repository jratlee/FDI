# The Go/No-Go Scorecard

Should you be in Davos next January? Most teams answer this with vibes. This
scorecard replaces vibes with a weighted score you can defend to a board.

Run it in one 45-minute session with the executive, the comms lead, and
whoever owns the budget. Score each factor 1 to 5, multiply by the weight, and
total. Be honest; a flattering score buys you an expensive, low-yield week.

## The factors

### 1. Strategic visibility goals (weight x5)

Does the January week map to a named business objective this year?

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | A live, named objective depends on the audiences that gather that week (raise, exit, policy position, category creation, global expansion) |
| 3 | Broad brand-elevation goals; senior audiences matter but no single objective depends on the week |
| 1 | The goal is "be seen at Davos." That is a cost, not a goal |

### 2. Audience fit (weight x4)

Are the people you need actually there, and reachable at your access level?

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Your top 20 target relationships (investors, policymakers, media, partners) reliably attend and you have warm paths to at least a third of them |
| 3 | The right categories of people attend, but you would be building the list cold |
| 1 | Your buyers and stakeholders are not there; you would be networking for its own sake |

### 3. Story readiness (weight x4)

Do you have something to say that survives that room?

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | A distinct, defensible point of view with proof, already publishing, that fits the year's global agenda |
| 3 | A good story that needs three to six months of development and pre-positioning |
| 1 | No point of view beyond the product pitch |

### 4. Budget reality (weight x3)

Score against the Budget Calculator output, not against hope.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | The all-in number fits inside existing budget without cannibalizing a working program |
| 3 | Fundable, but it displaces something that currently produces results |
| 1 | It requires new budget nobody has approved |

### 5. Calendar cost (weight x2)

The week costs far more than the week.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Principal can protect the week itself plus preparation time across the prior quarter |
| 3 | The week works but preparation will be squeezed |
| 1 | January is already committed; you would arrive unprepared |

### 6. Alternatives comparison (weight x2)

Score Davos against the best alternative use of the same money and time:
a major industry event, Cannes Lions, UNGA week, Web Summit, a self-hosted
executive dinner series, or a sustained thought-leadership program.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | No alternative concentrates your specific audiences the same way |
| 3 | One alternative is comparable at half the cost |
| 1 | An alternative clearly beats it for your goals |

## Scoring

Maximum score: 100.

| Total | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| 75 to 100 | Go. Move directly to the Twelve-Month Runway |
| 55 to 74 | Conditional go. Fix the lowest-weighted-score factor first (usually story readiness or audience paths), then re-score in 60 days |
| Under 55 | No-go this cycle. Redirect the budget to the strongest alternative and re-assess next year |

## The one-page recommendation

Whatever the outcome, write it up in five lines: the score, the top two
factors driving it, the decision, what would change it, and the budget
implication. That page is what makes the decision defensible in March when
someone asks why you did or did not go.

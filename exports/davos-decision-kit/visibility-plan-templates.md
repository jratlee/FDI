# The Visibility Plan Templates

Four working tools for the week itself: the meeting-request scripts, the
briefing-doc template, a model high-impact week, and the follow-up system.

## 1. Meeting-request scripts

The ask is always: specific, short, and anchored to the recipient's agenda,
not yours. Senior calendars for that week are set by November; send accordingly.

### Warm introduction (send to the connector)

> Subject: Intro to [Name] ahead of January?
>
> [Connector first name], I will be in Davos the week of [dates] and [Name] is
> at the top of my list. We are both working on [shared theme]; I published
> [anchor piece] on exactly this. Would you be open to a two-line
> introduction? Happy to send you the note to forward.

### Direct request (when you have a real hook)

> Subject: [Shared theme] in Davos, 20 minutes?
>
> [Name], your [talk / piece / decision] on [topic] lines up with what we are
> seeing in [your domain]: [one sharp sentence of your point of view]. I will
> be in Davos [dates]. Could we take 20 minutes over coffee near the
> promenade? I will come with one specific idea worth your time.

### Side-event host request

> Subject: [Your company] at [event name]
>
> [Host name], congratulations on this year's program. My work on
> [theme] fits your [session / dinner / forum] directly: [one sentence of
> proof, with a link to the anchor piece]. Is there room for me on the guest
> list on [date]? Happy to contribute [a short remark / data / a provocation]
> if useful.

## 2. The briefing doc (one page per day, prepared in December)

For each confirmed meeting:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Who | Name, role, why they matter to a named objective |
| Context | How you got the meeting; what they know about you already |
| Their agenda | What they are publicly working on or promoting that week |
| The one outcome | The single result that makes this meeting a success |
| The give | What you offer them: an insight, an introduction, data |
| The follow-up | What you will send within 72 hours, pre-drafted |

## 3. The model high-impact week

The pattern: mornings for scheduled meetings, afternoons for the promenade
and serendipity, evenings for hosted moments. Never schedule back-to-back;
everything runs late and the best conversations are unplanned.

| Day | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Sunday | Arrive, walk the promenade, confirm every meeting for the week by message |
| Monday | Anchor meetings 1 to 3. Evening: the highest-value dinner you have |
| Tuesday | Anchor meetings 4 to 6. Afternoon: side-event sessions on your theme. Evening: host or co-host your moment if you have one |
| Wednesday | The flex day: hold it open for the meetings that materialize mid-week; they are often the best ones |
| Thursday | Anchor meetings 7 to 9, closing conversations, promised introductions made in person |
| Friday | Depart. On the train or flight: write the capture log while it is fresh |

Daily discipline: ten minutes each night logging who you met, what was said,
what was promised, and what surprised you. Memory does not survive the week.

## 4. The follow-up system (where the ROI lives)

- **Within 72 hours:** every conversation gets its pre-drafted follow-up,
  personalized with one specific detail from the exchange. Include the thing
  you promised: the introduction, the data, the anchor piece
- **Within 2 weeks:** the synthesis note to your own leadership: what you
  learned, what it changes, what you are doing about it
- **Within 30 days:** second touch to the top ten relationships, moving each
  to a concrete next step: a call, a visit, a shared project
- **Within 90 days:** score the trip against the one-page recommendation from
  the Go/No-Go Scorecard, meeting by meeting

The test of the week is not the photos from the promenade. It is how many of
the top ten relationships are in motion by April.

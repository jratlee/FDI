# Top Call Executive Moves Model

This model turns executive-move monitoring from a company-specific tracker into an industry-wide signal system.

This model starts from a practical executive-move tracker pattern, then generalizes across the full advertising, marketing, media, commerce, retail, platform, agency, brand, analyst, and technology ecosystem.

## Why Exec Moves Matter

Executive moves are useful only when they are **validated by high-authority third-party sources or clearly identified primary sources** and reveal a broader signal, such as:

- a company changing growth strategy
- a platform investing in advertising, commerce, or AI
- an agency strengthening data, commerce, creative, media, or technology capabilities
- a brand changing marketing or comms direction
- a category hiring from another category, suggesting capability transfer
- a leadership exit amid performance pressure, reorganization, M&A, or strategic drift

Do not treat every appointment as equally important.

## Generalized Executive-Move Universe

The product should support three levels of coverage:

1. **Named watchlist:** known executives and companies provided by the user.
2. **Strategic role taxonomy:** roles that matter regardless of company.
3. **Industry universe:** companies across adjacent sectors where executive changes signal category movement.

### Medium Priority

Include only when company is important or context is strategic:

- COO
- CFO
- Chief Product Officer
- Chief Digital Officer
- Chief Data Officer
- Chief Customer Officer
- Chief Experience Officer
- Head of Loyalty
- Head of Creator / Influencer / Social

- Head of Privacy / Trust / Safety, if platform/ad relevant
- Regional president / market president
- VP / SVP Marketing
- VP / SVP Media
- VP / SVP Ad Sales
- VP / SVP Commerce

### Usually Low Priority

Exclude unless tied to a clear strategic story:

- HR / people roles
- legal / compliance roles
- procurement roles

- minor regional VP roles
- startup appointments with no category relevance
- board appointments unless linked to governance, M&A, activist pressure, or major strategy

## Strategic Interpretation Rules

For every included executive move, add one of these interpretations:

### Capability Build

The company is hiring for a missing or newly important capability.

Examples:

- hiring a retail media leader from Amazon/Walmart
- hiring an AI/product/data executive into marketing
- hiring a creator/influencer executive into a brand or agency

### Turnaround / Performance Pressure

The move follows weak sales, account losses, slowing growth, investor pressure, or restructuring.

### Category Convergence

The executive moves across sectors, suggesting capability transfer.

Examples:

- agency exec joins retailer media network
- CPG CMO joins platform
- tech product leader joins agency
- streaming ad exec joins retail media network

### Commercialization Shift

The company is monetizing data, inventory, audience, or commerce signals in a new way.

### Brand Repositioning

The move suggests a new brand, comms, creative, or customer-experience direction.

### M&A / Integration

The appointment supports acquisition integration, reorganization, or consolidation.

### Governance / Risk

The move relates to privacy, trust, safety, AI governance, regulatory, or transparency issues.

For exec moves, source log should include:

| Status | Executive | Company | Role | Source | URL | Rationale | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Confidence Rules

### High Confidence

- Company press release
- SEC filing / investor relations
- LinkedIn profile clearly updated
- reputable trade publication
- Reuters / CNBC / Wall Street Journal / Fortune

### Medium Confidence

- Business Wire / PR Newswire
- conference speaker bio
- credible newsletter
- agency or trade-body announcement

### Low Confidence

- SEO biography page
- outdated ranking pages
- scraped org chart
- unverified social post
- ambiguous LinkedIn snippet

Do not include low-confidence moves in the main brief unless clearly caveated.

## Third-Party Validation Rule

Preferred validation order:

1. Tier 1 business/news media or brand-name trade coverage.
2. Reputable category trade coverage.
3. Company press release / investor relations / official announcement for factual confirmation.
4. Business Wire / PR Newswire if clearly named and dated.
5. LinkedIn or conference bio only as supporting evidence, not sole validation unless the brief explicitly labels it as unconfirmed.

Reject or source-log only:

- scraped org charts;
- SEO bio pages;
- stale leadership pages;
- ambiguous search snippets;
- unknown newsletters;
- social posts without corroboration.

If no high-confidence executive moves surface after Tier 1 / brand-name trade search, do **not** stop. Use the current-week fallback ladder below.

## Current-Week Fallback Ladder

Do not leave the executive-moves section empty after one failed trade search. Use this ladder:

1. **Tier 1 / brand-name trade current-week coverage** — preferred.
2. **Specialist exec-move newsletters or indexes** — allowed if clearly labeled as specialist newsletter / lower than Tier 1, especially when they identify multiple moves and can guide confirmation.
3. **Company press releases / investor relations / Nasdaq / Business Wire / PR Newswire** — allowed as primary-source-only current-week moves when clearly labeled.
4. **Local user-provided newsletters/article exports** — allowed when the original publication/source is identified.
5. **Source-log-only leads** — scraped org charts, SEO bio pages, vague snippets, or social posts. Do not include in main brief.

When using fallback levels 2–4, label the item clearly:

```markdown
Source: [Company press release, Date](URL) — **Primary source; no Tier 1 third-party coverage found in this run**
```

or:

```markdown
Source: [CMO Ladder, Date](URL) — **Specialist exec-move newsletter; use as discovery / verify where possible**
```

## Paywall / Access Labeling

If a source appears paywalled, subscription-only, gift-gated, registration-gated, or only partially extracted, label it before the user clicks:

```markdown
Source: [Adweek, Date](URL) — **Paywalled / subscription-only**
```

```markdown
Source: [Ad Age, Date](URL) — **Gift article / may require registration**
```

```markdown
Source: [eMarketer, Date](URL) — **Paywalled / extraction limited**
```

# Top Call Source Authority Policy

Top Call briefs should be built from **validated, high-authority third-party sources** wherever possible.

The goal is not to collect the most links. The goal is to produce an executive-ready brief with sources a senior client, comms leader, analyst, or executive would trust.

## Core Rule

> Include only stories supported by Tier 1 / high-authority third-party media, brand-name trade publications, reputable analyst/trade bodies, or clearly identified primary sources used for factual confirmation.

Vendor blogs, SEO pages, generic trend posts, thin newsletters, and self-promotional sources should not drive the brief unless they are clearly labeled as lower-confidence context and corroborated elsewhere.

## Source Hierarchy

### Tier 1A — High-Authority Business / News Media

Use as strongest third-party validation.

Examples:

- Reuters
- Associated Press
- Bloomberg
- CNBC
- Wall Street Journal
- Financial Times
- New York Times
- Washington Post
- The Economist
- Fortune
- Forbes, selectively for reported pieces, not contributor SEO
- Business Insider, especially reported media/advertising coverage
- Variety, Hollywood Reporter, Deadline for media/entertainment
- Sports Business Journal for sports/media/brand partnerships

### Tier 1B — Brand-Name Advertising / Marketing / Media Trades

Use as primary category sources.

Examples:

- Ad Age
- Adweek
- Campaign
- The Drum
- Digiday
- Marketing Dive
- Retail Dive
- Modern Retail
- Retail Brew
- eMarketer / Insider Intelligence
- The Current
- MediaPost, selectively
- ExchangeWire, selectively for ad tech
- AdExchanger
- Marketing Brew
- Business of Fashion for fashion/beauty/luxury
- Restaurant Dive / Food Dive when relevant
- Glossy for fashion/beauty/retail

### Tier 1C — Reputable Analyst / Trade Body / Measurement Sources

Use for data, standards, forecast, and industry-positioning claims.

Examples:

- ANA
- IAB
- IAB Tech Lab
- 4A’s
- ARF
- WARC
- Forrester
- Gartner
- Nielsen
- Kantar
- Comscore
- Circana
- Conference Board
- eMarketer / Insider Intelligence
- Cannes Lions / LIONS official materials for event facts
- CES / NewFronts / Upfront official materials for event facts

### Tier 2 — Primary Sources for Fact Confirmation

Primary sources are useful but should not be the only source for strategic claims unless no third-party coverage exists.

Examples:

- company press releases
- investor relations pages
- official blogs
- SEC filings
- executive bios
- event speaker pages
- LinkedIn profiles
- Business Wire / PR Newswire

Use primary sources to confirm:

- appointment dates
- titles
- exact company language
- event details
- financial figures
- product names

But avoid letting company self-positioning become the strategic interpretation unless corroborated.

### Tier 3 — Supplemental / Discovery Only

Use only to find leads, background, or terminology. Do not include as main evidence unless corroborated by Tier 1 or Tier 2.

Examples:

- vendor blogs
- agency blogs
- SEO trend pages
- listicles
- generic “2026 trends” articles
- Substack/newsletter posts from unknown authors
- scraped org charts
- conference sponsor pages
- low-authority AI-generated summaries
- Wikipedia
- company-ranking pages without reporting

## Inclusion Rules

### Main Brief Inclusion

To include an item in the main brief, it should meet one of these standards:

1. Covered by at least one Tier 1A or Tier 1B source.
2. Published by a Tier 1C analyst/trade body with clear relevance.
3. Confirmed by a Tier 2 primary source **and** strategically important enough to include with a caveat.
4. Provided in local context by the user from a credible known source, with publication/source identified.

### Executive Moves Inclusion

Executive moves require higher source discipline.

Include if confirmed by:

- Tier 1A / 1B reported coverage;
- company press release or investor relations page;
- reputable trade publication;
- clearly updated LinkedIn profile plus corroborating company/event bio;
- Business Wire / PR Newswire for named public announcements.

Exclude or caveat if sourced only from:

- SEO bio pages;
- scraped org charts;
- ambiguous search snippets;
- outdated company pages;
- unknown newsletters;
- social posts without corroboration.

### Analyst / Report Claims

Numerical forecasts and market-size claims should come from:

- named analyst/research firms;
- reputable measurement companies;
- trade bodies;
- public filings;
- credible business media citing named reports.

Do not use unsupported vendor numbers unless clearly labeled as vendor-reported and not independently verified.

## Local Context Rules

Local context can include newsletters, article exports, PDFs, spreadsheets, and prior briefs. But the source authority still matters.

When reading local files:

1. Extract the original publication, author, date, and URL if present.
2. Classify the original source by tier.
3. If the local file does not identify a source, use it as context only, not as validation.
4. If local context includes paywalled Tier 1 article text or PDFs provided by the user, treat as high-authority user-provided source material.
5. If local context includes internal opinions or notes, cite them as internal context, not third-party validation.

## Source Log Requirement

Every brief should include a source log with authority classification.

| Status | Bucket | Source | Tier | Date | URL | Rationale | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Included | Retail media | Ad Age | Tier 1B | Date | URL | Brand-name trade, direct category relevance | High |
| Included | Exec moves | Company press release | Tier 2 | Date | URL | Confirms title/date; no Tier 1 coverage found | Medium |
| Excluded | CTV | Vendor blog | Tier 3 | Date | URL | SEO-like and not independently validated | Low |

## Confidence Labels

### High Confidence

- Tier 1A / 1B source with clear reporting.
- Tier 1C source for analyst/trade-body claim.
- Multiple independent sources align.

### Medium Confidence

- Tier 2 primary source confirms facts, but little third-party validation.
- Reputable trade body or company source, but strategic interpretation is ours.
- User-provided paywalled source with clear citation.

### Low Confidence

- Vendor blog, generic SEO content, unclear source, stale snippet, or unverified social/LinkedIn signal.
- Should generally be excluded from main brief.

## Rejected Source Examples

Reject or background-only:

- “Top 20 agencies in 2026” SEO listicles
- generic “marketing trends 2026” vendor posts
- scraped org charts
- Wikipedia entries
- personal blogs without category authority
- calendar pages or irrelevant search hits
- AI-generated news roundups without primary citations

## When Coverage Is Thin

Do not fill gaps with low-quality sources. But for **executive moves**, use the fallback ladder before declaring the section empty.

### General Buckets

Write:

> No high-confidence Tier 1 or validated third-party source surfaced for this bucket in this run. Recommend adding local newsletters, article exports, or a source-specific watchlist.

### Executive Moves

Use this fallback ladder:

1. Tier 1 / brand-name trade current-week coverage.
2. Specialist exec-move newsletters or indexes, clearly labeled.
3. Company press releases / investor relations / Nasdaq / Business Wire / PR Newswire, clearly labeled as primary-source-only.
4. Local user-provided newsletters/article exports with original publication identified.
5. Source-log-only leads for low-confidence material.

If using levels 2–4, include a clear access/source note so the user knows what they are clicking.


## Future Product Upgrade

Paid integrations can improve coverage later, but they are not required for the MVP.

Future options:

- media monitoring feeds
- premium trade feeds
- licensed analyst report repositories
- LinkedIn-approved people-data workflows
- Factiva / LexisNexis / Meltwater / Cision / Muck Rack style monitoring
- enterprise SharePoint/Google Drive connectors for licensed user-provided materials

These should be positioned as optional team/enterprise upgrades, not required for the base product.

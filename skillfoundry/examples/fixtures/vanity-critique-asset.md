# Why Reach Is the Wrong Metric — and What to Measure Instead

A CMO defending a 2026 budget needs to answer one question: what did this spend build that we still own?

For thirty years, marketing has been a bet on reach: buy impressions, count clicks, report engagement. That bet is over. Reach is commoditized. Impressions cost nothing to manufacture and prove nothing about intent. The platforms that sold you those clicks are now black boxes — you feed in a budget, and past that event horizon, light does not escape.

The CFO does not discount reach because they are unsophisticated. They discount it because it does not connect to anything on the P&L they control. What they track is retention, CAC, and the compounding value of an asset that earns next quarter without a new spend line.

The durable play is to build what you own: a persistent identity, a proprietary corpus, interfaces that other machines can query and trust. These compound. Reach does not.

Before your next planning cycle, audit one asset against a real value driver — not impressions delivered, but CAC it lowered or retention it kept. If you cannot name one, that is the gap.
